Across aquaculture farms, multiple ponds depend on electricity to keep aerators running.

[scene02]
When the power fails, the farmer must decide which ponds receive limited backup electricity.

[scene03]
Introducing AquaResQ, Team SKAY's explainable emergency intelligence and resource optimization system.

[scene04]
Each pond has its own planning profile, including water depth, starting oxygen, shrimp biomass and aerator requirements.

[scene05]
The outage simulator estimates how dissolved oxygen may change over time. Its oxygen-balance model produces trajectories that can be compared against a chosen protection threshold.

[scene06]
In our critical scenario, three aerators need six kilowatts, but the generator supplies only four. Over twelve hours, available energy is limited to forty-eight kilowatt-hours.

[scene07]
AquaResQ evaluates feasible whole-pond aerator allocations, checking generator power and available energy. It selects the combination that minimizes the modeled integrated oxygen deficit.

[scene08]
In this simulated example, AquaResQ reduces integrated oxygen deficit from eighty-nine point two eight to twenty-three point six — approximately seventy-four percent below the no-generator case.

[scene09]
Every recommendation is explainable. The system shows which ponds receive power, why alternatives were excluded, and the trade-offs between different allocation strategies.

[scene10]
Today, AquaResQ is a simulated planning prototype. Expert review and field validation come next.
